# AI Bias Recognition & Mitigation

| Bias Type | Description | Example | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Historical Bias | Bias inherited from past social patterns embedded in data. | A resume-screening model trained on ten years of company hiring data learns to favor men for engineering roles because the company historically hired far fewer women into those jobs. | Audit training data for historical skews, rebalance where appropriate, and add fairness checks before deployment. |
| Representation Bias | Some groups are underrepresented or misrepresented in the dataset. | A facial-recognition system performs well on light-skinned adult men but poorly on darker-skinned women because the training images contain too few examples of them. | Expand the dataset to include balanced coverage across demographics, contexts, and edge cases. |
| Measurement Bias | The labels or features used do not accurately capture the real-world concept. | A hospital risk model uses past healthcare spending as a proxy for medical need, causing Black patients to be rated as lower risk because they historically had less access to care and therefore lower recorded spending. | Use better target variables, validate proxies carefully, and test whether measurements differ systematically across groups. |
| Aggregation Bias | One model is used for populations that differ in important ways. | A speech-recognition model tuned mostly for standard American English performs worse for speakers with regional accents or for non-native English speakers. | Segment populations where needed, evaluate subgroup performance, and consider specialized or adaptive models. |
| Evaluation Bias | Benchmarks or test sets fail to reflect the real users or deployment context. | A skin-cancer detection model looks accurate on a benchmark built mostly from light-skin images, but accuracy drops in clinics serving patients with darker skin tones. | Build evaluation sets that mirror real deployment conditions and report subgroup-specific results. |
| Deployment Bias | A model is used in ways that differ from how it was designed or validated. | A tool built to support judges with structured risk information is treated in practice as an objective score that heavily drives bail decisions without human review. | Define clear use boundaries, train users, monitor real-world use, and require human oversight for high-stakes decisions. |
